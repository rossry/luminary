Map a scrambled sphere

The top view is the physical build: six boards and 33 panels plugged in with no organization — mixed 180/360-LED densities, random windings, unknown ports. The bottom view is the base station's window: what the plan intends. Both show the same broadcast — idle struts crawl with white beads, scrambled up top until the mapping unscrambles them.

Stage A — one planned board breathes below in its own color. Press ←/→ until the matching cluster breathes above, then Enter to lock that controller — locked boards hold their color steady.

Stage B — the panel under test plays the color wheel on its first and last quarters (a density mismatch shows as only one half lit). ←/→ pick the channel until the right panel lights; ↑ toggles density; ↓ toggles winding (wrong = the windmill sweeps the wrong way). One Enter records all three. Unmapped strips on the active board glow their first 30 LEDs as a hint.

WASD works everywhere, and P or space confirm just like Enter — the whole flow fits an alpha-only keyboard. Mapped boards flip to the descending rainbow ring when their last panel is confirmed; when the whole sphere is mapped, three quick waves, a blackout, and the spiral show wipes in from the apex.
